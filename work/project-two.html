DallyDeck

A card deck and companion app that takes the pressure off planning visits for UW students and their loved ones.

Role
UX Designer & Researcher
Timeline
Sep 2025 – Mar 2026
Tools
Figma, FigJam

Overview

Helping students spend less time on logistics and more time on shared memories.

Students who spend quality time with friends and family tend to have better mental well-being, but at UW, planning a visit is often a source of stress. Many students feel they don’t have the time or energy to figure out what to do with their guests.

DallyDeck is a hybrid physical card deck and mobile app for building personalized visits. Each card highlights a spot near campus or around Seattle, and a QR code links it to an app where students can search, save, and share cards with the people coming to see them.

I designed DallyDeck with Neel Sundar, Lauren Roberts, and Hannah Park across HCDE 302 and 303, a two-quarter upper-division series at the University of Washington.

Research Fall 2025

What we learned

Planning a visit is informal, last minute, and rarely about campus.

We ran four semi-structured interviews with UW undergraduates to understand the challenges they face when planning activities with friends and family who don’t go to UW.

  1. 01

    There are few UW-specific resources

    Students struggled to find activities for visitors that were tied to campus.

  2. 02

    Plans mostly happen off campus

    Without good on-campus ideas, students default to familiar spots elsewhere in Seattle.

  3. 03

    Getting around is a pain point

    Transportation and accessibility came up again and again.

  4. 04

    Planning is flexible and last minute

    Students rarely make detailed itineraries, so a rigid planner wouldn’t fit how they actually work.

Persona & journey map

Meet Dylan, an out-of-state student hosting family for a few days.

Dylan wants meaningful time with her family, but finding UW-specific activities is hard and logistics like transportation leave her overwhelmed. Mapping her journey from brainstorming to planning, communicating, and executing showed her mood dips sharpest during planning, when options run thin and logistics pile up.

Design Winter 2026

Design requirements

Five goals, with flexibility at the center.

We turned our research into requirements, and design reviews pushed us to support both careful planning and spontaneous exploration.

  • Inform

    Show students unique activities near campus.

  • Facilitate

    Make it easy to share plans between students and visitors.

  • Educate

    Introduce families to what UW and Seattle have to offer.

  • Empower

    Set students up to carry out their plans successfully.

Storyboards & information architecture

Reviewers loved the flexibility, so we paired the app with a physical deck.

Each of us storyboarded our most promising idea. In critique, reviewers suggested combining our digital journey-planning app with a physical card game, so we designed a system where the two work together. The cards are quick to browse, and the app adds current details and three paths: search for specific cards, save favorites, and share cards with friends.

Prototype & test Winter 2026

Low to mid fidelity

Testing early showed us where people got lost.

Four UW students tried a sketched card and digital wireframes. They saw the concept as genuinely useful but wanted a help section, weren’t sure whether cards covered UW or all of Seattle, and didn’t understand how collaboration worked. We addressed those in a mid-fidelity Figma prototype with detailed app screens and card mock-ups.

Usability testing & expert review

The cards worked. The app needed clearer feedback.

We ran usability tests with three participants alongside an expert review. People found the cards intuitive, attractive, and easy to scan, and they liked search and the QR code linking to the app. But icon meanings were unclear, building a deck was hard, and it wasn’t obvious when an action had worked.

What we changed

Clearer icons, simpler deck creation, confirmation feedback, and improved navigation.

What we left out

A collectible system that unlocked cards by visiting places. It could limit exploration, so we parked it as a future option.

Final design

A cohesive deck and app with a UW-inspired identity.

  • Branding

    A logo, UW-inspired colors, and a consistent icon set.

  • Easier cards

    Bigger images and labels with less text.

  • Clear pathways

    Labeled buttons, with scanning moved to the home screen.

  • Map & friends

    New pages to round out navigation.

Reflection

Designing across a physical and digital product.

Our biggest challenge was connecting a card deck and an app so moving between them felt natural. It also turned out to be what people responded to most, which taught me how much value there is in designing beyond a single screen. Testing early and often surfaced issues we would never have caught on our own.

Next, we’d raise the prototype’s fidelity, add scrollable decks and a shuffle option for suggestions, build more ways for hosts and visitors to plan together, and keep testing with students.
